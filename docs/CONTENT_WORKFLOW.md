# Content Workflow

## Scope

Signal Shelf covers English-language videos from U.S.-based creators and editorial institutions in three beats:

- AI & Tech
- Investment Banking
- Supply Chain

Company-owned channels are acceptable when the videos are editorial, educational, or reporting-led. Exclude channels whose primary purpose is selling subscriptions, promoting services, or routing viewers into a commercial funnel.

## Source selection

Audience size and demonstrated video reach are primary signals. Selection should consider:

- channel subscribers;
- median and top video views, not only a single viral upload;
- recent publishing activity;
- relevance to the beat;
- depth and clarity of the host or recurring editorial voice;
- whether the channel provides material worth turning into a substantial written briefing.

Low-view uploads can remain in the archive, but Daily Cut candidates should normally show meaningful reach relative to the channel and topic.

## Archive rules

- Archive start: January 1, 2026.
- Maximum: 365 videos per channel per calendar year.
- If a channel publishes more than 365 videos, retain the 365 highest-viewed videos.
- Store source upload date separately from the Daily Cut edition date.
- Do not label an unreviewed transcript as a completed briefing.

## Briefing standard

A briefing should let a reader understand the video's argument without watching the entire source while preserving a clear path back to the evidence.

Every published story should include:

1. A precise title that captures the central claim.
2. A lede explaining why the video matters.
3. Several substantive sections covering the argument, evidence, mechanism, and caveats.
4. A short “What to remember” list.
5. Timestamped links to the relevant parts of the source.
6. Accurate source metadata: channel, upload date, views, source duration, and reading time.

Avoid shallow recaps, generic bullet lists, invented certainty, and promotional language. Write in natural English and attribute claims to the speaker or source when the video does not independently establish them.

## Review checklist

- Transcript reviewed from beginning to end.
- Names, numbers, dates, and quotations checked against the source.
- Summary distinguishes the speaker's claims from verified facts.
- Important counterarguments or limitations are represented.
- Timestamps open the correct evidence.
- No unsupported claims are introduced.
- Final copy is readable aloud without tables, URLs, or abrupt fragments inside narration text.

These are internal editorial checks. They should not appear as badges or labels inside the published article.

## Narration writing

Narration is generated from the title, lede, section headings, section paragraphs, and takeaways. For natural delivery:

- use complete sentences;
- vary sentence length;
- put punctuation where a presenter would pause;
- avoid dense parentheticals and repeated symbols;
- spell out ambiguous abbreviations when necessary;
- listen to the regenerated file after material copy changes.
